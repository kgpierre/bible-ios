# iPhone Duo adaptive reader

27 September 2026. Owner request: hypothesize useful Duo features, begin full-feature support with an iPad-like inner reader and landscape page swiping, test it, and update AGENTS.md.

## Design and SDK evidence

Apple's [Prepare your app for iPhone Duo](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/tech-talks/111461/) describes regular size classes on the inner display, adaptive native bars, and asymmetric safe areas. [Strike a pose with adaptive layouts](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/tech-talks/111463/) describes reserved division/occlusion regions and arrangements. These are API/design sources, not evidence that this app passes the corresponding device tests.

The owner's expanded Xcode reports **27.1 (27A9269)** and includes the iOS Simulator 27.1 SDK. Commands use a per-process `DEVELOPER_DIR`; the installed system Xcode is unchanged. The app retains minimum iOS 26.0.

## Feature hypotheses

1. **A pocket Bible that opens into facing pages.** Outer-display scrolling continues at the same passage on the inner display. The owner subsequently requested automatic facing-page swiping in the partially folded book pose. This now uses the public active division region, centers the native spread on the fold, and reserves an inner text margin. It temporarily overrides the flat-screen choice without changing it. Appearance has an enabled-by-default opt-out for continuous scrolling/selection. **Superseded 31 October 2026 (owner):** the opt-out is removed and book layout is always on; continuous scrolling remains for accessibility text sizes and pages too narrow to read. Two Pages is also the default flat landscape layout (preference payload version 2 resets a version 1 page layout to Two Pages once).
2. **A source companion beside Scripture.** In a suitable folded arrangement, show the selected verse's existing source notes on the other region. Authoritative Scripture stays visually separate. This reuses sourced notes; it does not introduce commentary or generated Scripture. Proposed, not implemented.
3. **A stable reading surface in tabletop pose.** The initial implementation places the reader above an active horizontal division and chapter controls below it when both regions fit. The live horizontal-division UI test passed after rotating a partially folded Duo; a physical 90-degree tabletop review remains outstanding. An optional source-note companion remains a future proposal.

Continuity is the baseline requirement, not a reason to add a new mode or interrupt reading with a fold prompt. No new accounts, network services, telemetry, or corpus changes are needed.

## Initial implementation

- The existing regular-size-class system-tab layout already admits a regular-width phone. It retains all reader, Saved, Search, notes, annotation, appearance, and overview routes.
- Remove the `.pad` restriction from the page preference. It is also configurable while a foldable phone is closed, and persists normally.
- Replace the 960-point spread cutoff with a content policy: at least 420 points per page at default type, scaled with Dynamic Type, a landscape-shaped usable viewport, regular width, and enough vertical space. This is a provisional readability budget, not a hardcoded Duo screen dimension. Accessibility categories use the continuous reader.
- Keep the existing native page curl, verse-boundary pagination, semantic anchors, and independent local annotations. No storage or Scripture changes.
- On iOS 27.1, query active `GeometryProxy.reservedRegions(kind: .division)`, including their margins. Classify a vertical spanning division as book pose and a horizontal spanning division as tabletop. No model identifiers, guessed angles, or timer polling. Older systems return no fold layout.
- Book mode uses the actual division midpoint, accounting for asymmetric available space, and adds an inner margin to each native text page. The measuring view uses the same reduced text width as the visible pages. Automatic book mode falls back at accessibility text sizes or insufficient page width.
- Any scrolling fallback while a division is active (automatic book layout off, accessibility text, pages too narrow, or a tabletop split that does not fit) places the continuous reader in `ReaderFoldLayout.clearPane`: the larger side of a vertical division, or above a horizontal one whenever that side is at least 200 points or the larger side. Text never spans the division. Chapter controls remain in More and the passage selector. Added after the 27 September codebase audit; covered by layout unit tests, not yet by Device Hub pose transitions.
- Book pose has a separate 360-point default page budget (a narrower reading column plus gutter/margins), scaled with Dynamic Type. The first actual-pose test exposed that the 420-point flat-layout budget rejected usable book pages beside the native side toolbar. The observed layout had 371.5 points per page after fold clearance. These measurements are regression inputs, not device-name or screen-size detection.
- A persisted `automaticBookLayout` option defaults to true when absent from an older preference payload; it does not reset any existing appearance values. No user-database migration. Removed 31 October 2026: the stored key is ignored on decode.

## 6 November 2026: bottom clearance and outer-display side rail

Owner refinement: on standard iPhones, leave more room between the end of the text and the floating bottom navigation; on the Duo presentation that already stacks the top controls in a vertical right-side capsule, do the same, move Read/Saved/Search to the right as a vertical stack, and put the passage button on the right too.

- **Interpretation.** The screenshot is the Duo outer display: compact width, the system status bar and toolbar stacked in a column at the trailing edge. On the iPhone Duo simulator (iOS 27.2) that display reports a 382 x 644 point safe area with 0 leading, 84 trailing, 0 top and 34 bottom insets and a compact horizontal size class. The side rail is chosen by `ReaderLayout.sideRailWidth`: a trailing safe-area inset of at least 60 points with at most 20 leading, and not at accessibility text sizes. This is derived only from live asymmetric safe areas (no device name, orientation or `UIScreen`), so symmetric landscape phones, portrait phones, regular-width iPad and the inner display are unchanged. A column on the leading side (a rotated outer display) is not handled and keeps the bottom bars; that presentation was not observed.
- **Vertical bar (superseded the custom rail, 7 November 2026).** Where the system reports a vertical bar (`UITraitCollection.verticalBarEdge`, read by `VerticalBarProbe`), the app uses the system `TabView`, whose tab bar joins the status bar and toolbar in that column with native selection, drag, and Dynamic Island handling; the compact (outer display) reader keeps its text layout. This replaced a custom rail positioned from safe-area widths, which collided with the system toolbar when the Dynamic Island expanded and needed a hand-built drag lens. The reader's top controls are one custom toolbar item (`SideLensStack`) the system still places in the column: touching lifts a tinted glass lens behind the control under the finger, the lens follows a drag, and releasing over a control activates it; a tap is left to the control, a release more than 28 points outside cancels, the lens resets if the system cancels the touch, and touches starting on More go to its menu. The passage control floats bottom trailing and shares one capsule with Read Aloud's player, moving controls that don't fit into the player's menu. In the vertical bar, XCTest sees the tabs as buttons, not a tab bar element. Without a vertical bar, compact windows keep 2a's bottom row.
- **Clearance.** `ReaderLayout.chapterEndClearance` (scrolling reader's bottom text inset beyond the safe-area chrome) rises from 32 to 72 points. Measured on an iPhone 18 Pro simulator at the end of Isaiah 1, the final line now rests about 48 points above the bar capsules (about 30 before the first iteration). Book pages keep their existing 8-point band. Text still scrolls under the floating glass mid-chapter by design; the clearance applies when scrolled to the end.
- **Validated (simulators only).** Debug build with no new warnings; iPhone Duo simulator (iOS 27.2) outer display screenshots before and after; iPhone 18 Pro end-of-chapter screenshot; `ReaderLayoutTests` including a new side-rail predicate test; `testBooksSegmentsAndCollapsingNavigation`, `testCompactPortraitPhoneWithoutHingeHidesPageAndFoldSettings` and `NarrationUITests` on iPhone 18 Pro (`testScrollingReaderFollowsReadAloud` timed out once at the 45-second narration wait, then passed on rerun).
- **Not tested.** The side rail with live UI automation on the Duo simulator; the inner display flat, book and tabletop poses (not run after this change); the rail with the Read Aloud mini player active; Dynamic Type sizes near the accessibility boundary in the rail; keyboard focus order in the rail; Split View; any physical device.

### Addendum, 7 November 2026: landscape iPhone bottom controls

Owner report (iPhone landscape, Psalms 25): the passage capsule floated mid-height over verse 2, the tab capsule overlapped verse 3, and the two were not aligned. Cause: an iPhone 18 Pro in landscape is regular width, so it took the iPad path (system `TabView` with its own bottom tab bar, plus the trailing passage capsule in a bottom inset above that bar). The path is now chosen by `ReaderLayout.usesTopTabLayout`: the top-tab layout needs regular width and regular height. Compact height (landscape phones) uses the 2a bottom row, with the passage capsule and tabs adjacent on one baseline. Chosen over a trailing column because the row is the owner's selected 2a arrangement and a trailing stack would eat reading width on a short, wide screen. In compact height the tab region is bounded (280 points) so the floating tab capsule does not centre itself far from the passage capsule, the bar height is 56 points, and an opaque canvas band sits behind the row (through the bottom safe area) so text stops at the controls' top edge instead of scrolling under the glass. Horizontal safe areas (Dynamic Island, home indicator) come from the system safe-area inset. The Duo side rail is never taken at compact height. Fold detection is untouched, but a regular-width window with compact height now gets the 2a row; a Duo posture that reports compact height would also take it (not observed, not tested).

Validated on the iPhone 18 Pro simulator (iOS 27.0) in landscape-left and landscape-right (screenshots after the change show one baseline, no overlap at rest). The two orientations looked identical in the simulator, which does not draw the Dynamic Island; the island and home-indicator clearance relies on system safe areas and is untested on hardware. Not tested: landscape at accessibility text sizes, landscape with Read Aloud's mini player, landscape end-of-chapter spacing, landscape on iPad or Duo.

### Outer-display rail refinements — 7 November 2026 (owner requests)

- **Icon-only destinations:** Read/Saved/Search are icons only, on the Duo rail only. VoiceOver labels, Large Content Viewer, and pointer help still name each one. The rail never appears at accessibility sizes, where the bottom bars keep visible labels.
- **Uniform widths:**
  - The passage control and the destinations share one 48 pt width.
  - They are centered 36 pt in from the trailing column's edge, to match the system's vertical toolbar pill.
  - Both values were measured on the iPhone Duo outer display simulator (iOS 27.2), because the system pill's frame isn't available to the app. Recheck them on hardware.
- **Liquid Glass:** the selection is a tinted glass lens with a shared `glassEffectID`, so it morphs between destinations, including after ⌘1/2/3. A pressed destination shows its own clear glass lens and a slight scale.
  - The capsule itself is not interactive. When it was, a press lit up all three destinations as one button (owner feedback, 7 November 2026). Reduce Motion moves it without animation. The standard iPhone bar is already a system `UITabBar` and was unchanged.
- **Rotated outer display:** the rail now also requires a portrait usable size at least 560 pt tall. A rotated, short outer display keeps the bottom row, instead of the rail colliding with the system toolbar and the camera cutout.
- **Evidence:**
  - `DuoRailUITests` passed on the iPhone Duo simulator, outer display. It checks that each destination switches and becomes selected, keeps its name, and has a 44 pt target, and that the passage control and the destinations share one width. It skips on devices without the rail.
  - Screenshots of the rail, the Saved lens, and the rotated bottom row were inspected.
- **Not verified:** the morph animation itself (screenshots show only the end states), dark mode, Reduce Transparency and Increase Contrast, and the alignment on physical hardware.

### Presentations and SDK findings — 7 November 2026

- **Appearance and Summary** share one presentation: a popover beside the top controls on regular width (iPad, inner display), a sheet with medium and large detents on compact width. Both point at an app-placed anchor rather than the system toolbar button: in the inner display's vertical bar, UIKit sometimes couldn't resolve the button and fell back to the window's top-left corner, on the left page. In a wide window the popover is no wider than the trailing page beside the bar (about 347 points on the inner display simulator), so it stays on the right page.
- **Outer display while unfolded:** an opt-in Appearance setting ("Show Chapter on Outer Display", under Foldable, off by default) offers the book and chapter heading, never Scripture text, as iOS 27's `ExternalNonInteractiveAccessory`. The system decides whether it appears: on the unfolded Duo simulator (iOS 27.2) it reported the accessory unavailable, and the setting's footer says so while that's true. The camera-capture accessory applies only during camera capture. Verify on hardware.
- **27.1 APIs not yet adopted:** `UIBarButtonItem.axisBehavior`, `verticalBarCompressionBehavior` (the system default prefers the tab bar), and `UIArrangementViewController` split and overlay arrangements for a future companion pane.
